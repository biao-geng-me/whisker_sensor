ESP32 board ESP32 DevKit V1, 30-pin (DOIT layout) · top view, antenna up

Pin order of the common DOIT ESP32 DevKit V1 30-pin board, with its silkscreen labels (D23 = GPIO23, RX2 = GPIO16, TX2 = GPIO17). Clones occasionally differ: check the labels printed on your board before soldering. The six P2 wires land on six adjacent left-row pins in P2 order: P2 pin 1–6 → D32, D33, D25, D26, D27, D14, so one straight 6-wire cable with a 1×6 housing at each end fits (firmware since 2026-10-27; older firmware used a different order, and the wiring must match the firmware). The P1 wires are all on the right row. The board’s GND pins are both near the USB end (left pin 14, right pin 14), so the P1 ground wire starts at the right-row GND, just below D15.

  • Neither header has a GND pin. The ESP32 ground must still be tied to the PCB ground. The through-hole GND points are MD1 pin 2 (the middle lead of the 3-pin 3.3 V buck module just right of J1; recommended, ~8 mm from P1) and J1 pin 4 (plus the J1 shell tabs). Mounting holes MH1–4 are not connected. See Ground wiring below for how to route it.
  • Mux 1 (SEL*_1) is the lower CD4097B, the one carrying OUT1–16 for J2/J3; mux 2 (SEL*_2) is the upper one, OUT17–32 for J4/J5. Careful: the silkscreen text “MUX1” sits next to the upper chip and “MUX2” next to the lower one, the opposite of the net names. Go by the nets. Select lines A, B, C go to CD4097B pins 10, 11, 14; the firmware writes the 3-bit select value to both muxes at once.
  • INA351 REF divider, two copies. All 32 INA351 REF pins (pin 5) share one net, AMP_REF_3V3, set by R7 (3V3 → REF) and R8 (REF → GND) with C4 (1 µF) to GND. MAIN.SchDoc places the Amplifier_Reference sheet twice, so the layout has two identical copies (top and bottom edge, just right of the muxes) in parallel on that one net; one copy is enough. The schematic values (100 k / 100 k, REF ≈ 1.65 V) would rail every channel, since the ADC full scale is ±1.2 V with AINxN at GND. On this board (bench, 2026-10-21) only the top copy (above the upper mux, between J5 and J4) is fitted, with C4, R7 and R8. R7 had an intermittent joint (it read 45 k or about 100 k depending on the probing; reflowed, it now reads ≈46 k). R8 did not conduct: powered, REF sat at 3.28 V, pulled to 3V3 by R7 alone; removed on 2026-10-21, it measured open (a cracked part). So before the reflow REF floated on C4 (both resistors effectively open), and with R8 open every channel railed. R8 was then replaced with a 10 k: REF reads 0.294 V, stable, against 0.59 V predicted by 3.3 × 10/(46 + 10); an extra ~8–13 k to GND is unexplained (the part checks 10 k off-board). The bottom copy is empty. Target: REF ≈ 0.58 V with R7 = 47 k, R8 = 10 k (any 0603 1 % parts), which puts a balanced 352 Ω gauge at mid-range.
  • P1 net names are Raspberry Pi header numbers. The layout calls the P1 nets PIN23 (SCLK), PIN21 (MISO), PIN19 (MOSI), PIN07 (GPCLK0 → CLKIN), PIN27 and PIN29: the 40-pin Pi header positions. The board was laid out to be driven by a Pi; the ESP32 wiring above replaces that.
  • CLKIN and SCLK are the fast lines. Keep the P1 wires short (under ~10 cm), use a multi-pin housing or solder, and add 33–100 Ω series resistors at the ESP32 end of SCLK, CLKIN, DIN and CS before raising the clocks.

Mux pinouts (CD4097B)

Each CD4097B is a dual 8-to-1 switch: the select lines A/B/C (from P2) pick the same position on both halves at once, so Xk connects to COM X and Yk to COM Y, and each COM goes to one ADC channel. Every OUT net is one INA351 output after its 1 k / 0.1 µF filter. Top view, pin 1 at the top left (the dot on the board view). Mux 1 is the lower chip (silkscreen “MUX2”). SOIC-24 at 1.27 mm pitch: the leads are easy to touch with a sharp meter probe.

  • Suspect: mux 1 X6 (pin 3, OUT6 = W3_B) and Y6 (pin 16, OUT16 = W8_B). With no gauge plugged in, an INA351 output sits near its 3.3 V rail and the channel should read “open” (≥1200 mV). These two read −110 mV and ~1 mV instead, which an INA output cannot produce, so the ADC sees an undriven input on select 6 of mux 1. W3_B also jumped while sensors were hot-swapped. Both are position 6 on the same chip, while select 6 works on the other half of the scan (if SELA/SELB/SELC were faulty, other channels would be wrong too), so the likely causes are the two mux pins (open solder joints on pins 3 and 16) or the OUT6/OUT16 nets upstream (INA351 or filter).
  • Check (powered, no gauge in W3 B or W8 B): measure pin 3 and pin 16 of mux 1 to GND. About 3.2 V on the pin means the INA side is fine and the fault is the joint or the mux; then press the lead gently with the probe while streaming and watch whether the channel jumps to “open”. Near 0 V or floating on the pin means the fault is upstream on the OUT net.

Ground wiring: ESP32 to PCB

The ESP32 needs one ground wire to the PCB. Take it from MD1 pin 2, the middle lead of the 3.3 V buck module (it sticks out on the bottom side), which is the through-hole GND closest to P1. What matters is its route: at the MHz edges of SCLK and CLKIN, each signal’s current returns along the ground path, and the area between the signal wires and that path is a loop that radiates, picks up noise and rings. Route the ground wire with the P1 wires so the loop is just the gap between adjacent wires.

Bench results, 2026-10-21/22: use exactly one ground wire, and the best one is the wire from MD1 pin 2 twisted with CLKIN. Both twisted wires connected at once (MD1 pin 2 → both ESP32 GND pins, one twisted with CLKIN, one with SCLK): CRC and stale counters climbed continuously (tens of thousands), a ground loop through the ESP32 board. The original single wire (supply negative → one ESP32 GND, routed away from the signal wires): clean on 2026-10-21, but on 2026-10-22 after the wires had moved it gave ~60 CRC errors/s (74 Hz scan), sensitive to hand position. Only the CLKIN-twisted wire: CRC stays at 0, scan 75.9 Hz, and the noise between the two gauges of one whisker is back in phase. The return current of the clock then flows right beside it, which keeps the loop small and the crosstalk onto SCLK low. The SCLK-twisted wire alone was not tested.

Now ground branched off at the supply

Recommended ground wire runs with P1

  • One ground wire, MD1 pin 2 to the ESP32 GND. Solder it to the middle lead of MD1 where it sticks out on the bottom side (J1 pin 4 works too, but is farther from P1), not out at the supply. Its neighbors 2.54 mm away are VIN and 3V3: avoid bridges, cover the joint with heat-shrink or Kapton, and check it reads ~0 Ω to J1 pin 4 and no short to the outer leads. On the DevKit V1 use the right-row GND just below D15, the same side as the P1 pins.
  • Keep it against the P1 wires the whole way. Tape or twist it into the P1 bundle. Even better: two ground wires from MD1 pin 2, one twisted with SCLK (D18) and one with CLKIN (RX2/GPIO16), ending at the two ESP32 GND pins (both are tied together on the board). DRDY, CS, DIN and DOUT can run loosely alongside.
  • Remove the old branch from the supply lead. Leaving it in parallel keeps a second, large ground loop.
  • Short is still the main rule: P1 wires under ~10 cm, soldered or in a multi-pin housing. P2 (mux selects) changes only once per mux step and is less critical.
  • Earth loop check: the ESP32 is also grounded through the laptop’s USB. A floating bench supply output is fine; if the supply ties its negative to earth and the laptop is on its charger, mains hum can enter through that loop. Running the laptop on battery is a quick test.

Read before wiring

  • Pin pattern is the same on all four headers. Pin 1 is the square pad at the bottom of the right-hand column; odd pins run up the right column, even pins up the left. Gauges take pins 1+3, 2+4, 5+7, 6+8 and so on. The 3V3 and sense pins alternate in a checkerboard, so a gauge never shares a pin with its neighbor.
  • Gauge polarity does not matter. A gauge is a single resistor between 3V3 and the sense node (352 Ω R3/R6 to GND completes the half bridge). Either wire can go on either pin of its pair.
  • The odd CONN numbers do not exist on the PCB. In the schematic CONN1, CONN3, … are labels on the 3V3 pins; in the layout they are merged into the 3V3 net. Only CONN2, 4, … 64 appear as nets.
  • Labels are the firmware / wavi_py names. Whisker n gauge A is OUT(2n−1) and gauge B is OUT(2n), as in whisker_link/channels.py. On each header, whisker numbers count up from the pin-1 end, A is the right-hand column and B the left. A bench test (J2 pins 1–4 show as W1 in wavi_py) confirms this.
