MSD P25937 · whisker PCB rev 2 · one whisker, end to end

Whisker signal chain

How one whisker (W1, gauges A and B) travels through the board: quarter bridge, INA351 amplifier, RC filter, CD4097B mux and ADS131M04 ADC to the ESP32 and the host. The other 15 whiskers are identical copies on other mux inputs. For where things plug in, see the header map.

Per gauge

  • Quarter bridge. The gauge (in the whisker) runs from 3V3 to the sense node, R3 (352 Ω) from the sense node to GND. A fixed arm R1/R2 makes 1.65 V. Only the sense pin carries signal; every odd header pin is the same 3V3 net, so gauge orientation does not matter.
  • INA351 (GS = GND, gain 10) amplifies the difference of the two arms and adds REF. Higher gauge resistance → lower sense voltage → higher output.
  • RC filter 1 k / 0.1 µF (≈1.6 kHz) on each output, then the net OUTn goes to a mux input. W1 gauge A = OUT1 = mux #1 X3, gauge B = OUT2 = mux #1 X4; both reach the ADC on AIN2 (COM_X1), at mux selects 3 and 4.
Vsense = 3.3 V × 352 / (352 + Rgauge)
OUT = 10 × (1.65 V − Vsense) + REF ≈ REF + 23 mV per Ω above 352 Ω

Shared parts

  • REF (AMP_REF_3V3) is one net for all 32 INA351s, set by R7/R8 with C4. It sets where zero bridge signal sits on the output. The ADC measures OUT against GND (AINxN grounded), so REF drift shows on every channel at once. The layout has two copies of the divider (the schematic places the Amplifier_Reference sheet twice): pads at the top and bottom board edges, wired in parallel onto that one net, so either copy sets REF. As built (bench, 2026-10-21): bottom copy empty; top copy R7 ≈ 46 k (joint reflowed), C4 fitted, R8 replaced with 10 k (the original part measured open, which had REF at 3.28 V and every channel railed). REF now reads 0.294 V, stable; 3.3 × 10/(46 + 10) predicts 0.59 V, so something adds ~8–13 k to GND (unexplained, accepted).
  • Mux and ADC. Two CD4097B (X and Y sections each) feed the four ADC inputs: mux #1 X → AIN2, Y → AIN3 (OUT1–16); mux #2 X → AIN0, Y → AIN1 (OUT17–32). Each mux has its own three select lines (P2), and the firmware sets both to the same select, so 8 selects × 4 ADC channels = 32 gauges per scan.
  • ADC ADS131M04: ±1.2 V full scale, gain 1, internal 1.2 V reference, OSR 128 at CLKIN 0.8 MHz (from ESP32 GPIO16) → 320 µs per conversion. Per select the firmware settles 250 µs, drops 3 conversions and keeps the 4th (sinc3 filter flushed), ≈1.6 ms per select, ≈76 scans/s.
  • Power: 5.5 V bench supply → J1 → MD1 buck → 3V3 for the bridges, amplifiers, muxes and ADC. The ESP32 is powered by USB and shares ground through exactly one wire. Best on the bench (2026-10-22): the wire from MD1 pin 2 twisted with CLKIN (CRC 0, 75.9 scans/s). Two ground wires at once gave continuous CRC errors (a ground loop); a loose wire routed away from the signals gave ~60 CRC errors/s, depending on where it lay.

Noise: where the 6–7 Hz tone came from (bench, 2026-10-21/22)

A ~0.2–0.3 mV tone at a wandering 6–7 Hz showed on the channels with a whisker or test resistor. Raw burst captures (one mux select held, every conversion kept, 3125 / 3906 / 1953 SPS from three CLKINs) put its true frequency at 845 Hz: the only frequency consistent with all three rates. 845 Hz lies just above 11 × 76.2 Hz, so the 76 Hz scan folds it to ~6.8 Hz, and a 0.5 % drift at the source moves the folded tone by several Hz.

  • Mechanism. The 845 Hz is ripple on the board 3V3 (~2.1 mV). Both bridge arms (gauge/R3 and R1/R2) are fed from 3V3, so ripple cancels in a balanced bridge and passes in proportion to the imbalance: tone ≈ (OUT − REF) × ripple / 3.3 V, i.e. 0.63–0.66 mV per volt of offset (burst 4, both points on one line through REF). The inline 22 Ω resistors unbalanced their bridges by ~0.4 V; that is why the tone followed the cable. Hands on the cable changed nothing (conducted, not picked up).
  • Consequence. Keep gauges near balance at rest (drop the inline 22 Ω where the gauge alone sits near 352 Ω): a balanced channel carries ~0.02 mV of it. Under bending the ripple scales with the signal (~0.07 % of the swing), which is harmless.
  • Left over (equal on all channels, ~0.02–0.05 mV): lines at 125 Hz and harmonics, 437.5, 500 and 1000 Hz, likely ESP32 activity on the ground. The 845 Hz source on 3V3 (MD1 or a load on the rail) is not identified; it needs a scope.
  • Wiring matters as much: one ground wire, twisted with CLKIN, is the only configuration with CRC 0 (see the header map). CRC retries jitter the select timing and scramble the phase of folded tones between channels.

How the ADC turns P − N into a signed number

The ADS131M04 is a delta-sigma converter. It never takes an absolute value and flips the sign: it continuously balances the input difference against ±1.2 V, and the sign falls out of that balance.

  1. Sample the difference. A small capacitor is switched between AINxP and AINxN, so it holds a charge proportional to P − N. Only the difference is stored, which is why each pin may sit anywhere from about −1.3 V to AVDD (3.3 V) while only P − N is limited to ±1.2 V.
  2. Accumulate. The charge, minus the feedback from the previous step, is added to an integrator (a running total).
  3. Decide one bit. A comparator looks at the sign of the total: positive gives 1 and subtracts +1.2 V worth of charge next clock, negative gives 0 and adds 1.2 V worth. This repeats every modulator clock (CLKIN / 2, 400 kHz now).
  4. Average the bits. To keep the integrator near zero, the average feedback must equal the input, so the fraction of 1s encodes P − N: fraction = (1 + Vin / 1.2 V) / 2. The sinc3 filter turns the bits into one signed 24-bit result every 128 bits (OSR 128), 3125 per second now; each result is a weighted average of the last 3 × 128 bits.

Everything scales with CLKIN, which the ESP32 generates. At the datasheet’s nominal 8.192 MHz the same OSR 128 gives 31.25 µs per result (32 kSPS). The sinc3 shape is fixed; OSR and CLKIN are the only knobs.

Worked example, bit by bit

The simplest loop that shows the idea: a first-order modulator (one integrator) and a plain count of the 1s (a flat average over one conversion period, i.e. sinc1). Each row is one modulator clock. The comparator looks at the integrator before the step; the feedback is +1.2 V for a 1 and −1.2 V for a 0; the integrator adds input − feedback. The count estimate after n bits is 1.2 V × (2 × 1s / n − 1). OSR 32 and OSR 64 are the same bit stream, counted for 32 or 64 bits (highlighted rows). The chip itself never goes below OSR 128 (64 in turbo mode); 32 and 64 keep the tables short.

  • The count is exact once the integrator is added back. The integrator holds everything the feedback has not yet balanced: input = average feedback + (integrator at the end − integrator at the start) / OSR. The last column applies this and recovers the input to rounding. The count’s error is just the leftover in the integrator divided by OSR.
  • So doubling OSR only halves the worst case. The integrator stays within a couple of volts, so a plain count is good to about ±2.4 V / OSR: ~75 mV at OSR 32, ~38 mV at 64. The actual error depends on where the integrator happens to stop. For −0.314567 V both counts land on −0.3000 V: the longer count did not help this time.

Why the real chip does much better

Two improvements, both inside the ADS131M04: a higher-order loop (more integrators) pushes the quantization error to high frequencies, and the sinc3 filter weights the bits with a smooth hump over 3 × OSR instead of counting flatly, which removes that high-frequency error far better. The table gives the worst error over many successive results, from idealized noise-free simulations of a first- and second-order loop (the datasheet does not publish the exact loop; second order stands in for “higher order”).

Notes for this board

  • Out of range. Beyond ±1.2 V the ±1.2 V feedback can no longer balance the input, the stream becomes all 1s (or 0s) and the code saturates at 0x7FFFFF (0x800000). That is the “1200 mV / open” reading of an unconnected channel: its INA351 output heads for 3.3 V, legal for the pin but beyond 1.2 V of difference.
  • Why 3 results are discarded after a mux switch. Each result’s filter window spans 3 conversion periods, so the first results after a switch still contain bits from the previous channel. The firmware waits 250 µs, then keeps the 4th result that completes, whose window lies entirely after the switch. A faster CLKIN shortens the window proportionally.
  • On this board AINxN is tied to GND and AINxP gets the amplifier output (0 to ~3.3 V), so only the 0 to +1.2 V half of the range is used; the amplifier cannot go below 0 V.
  • Better wiring would tie AINxN to REF: the same machinery would then measure OUT − REF, signed, over the full ±1.2 V, and REF drift would cancel in hardware.
